How the application works

Timelog is used to declare on-call duty and overtime. Employees declare per month, the line manager validates, and Finance receives one aggregated monthly export.

  1. The employee fills a draft for the month: on-call days and, for normal employees, overtime entries. The two sheets are separate drafts.
  2. One Submit sends every draft of that month to the line manager together (with a comment and, if enabled, a signature).
  3. The manager approves, or rejects with a comment. A rejected sheet is edited and submitted again.
  4. Approved sheets appear in the monthly reports that IT Procurement sends to Finance (Payroll).

The month shown on the On-call and Overtime pages is kept in sync until you change it. The Current month button jumps back to today's month.

Roles & visibility

RoleWhat they can do
EmployeeDeclares on-call and overtime, submits, withdraws a submitted sheet, sees only their own data.
ExpertOn-call only. An intervention on an on-call day doubles that day's rate. Experts cannot file overtime (refused server-side).
Line managerAnyone with direct reports. Approves or rejects the sheets of their direct reports and, skip-level, of everyone below them (for example when the direct manager is absent), sees everyone below them in the hierarchy and has the Statistics page (status and totals of their team).
IT ProcurementDownloads the on-call and overtime reports for the whole organisation under Payroll, sends them to Finance, and records here that they were sent. Cannot see individual declarations. Assigned in Users (or by the LDAP group LDAP_GROUP_PROCUREMENT).
AdministratorManages users, grades, holidays, settings and sees the audit log. Can approve any sheet and reopen an approved sheet (audited).

Colleagues never see each other's declarations. Opening a record you are not allowed to see answers 404, so its existence is not disclosed. The line manager and the team come from the directory (AD manager and department attributes) at each login, unless you lock them in Users.

Preparing LDAP users in advance: Users › Prepare LDAP users creates directory accounts before the first sign-in from a list of usernames, optionally looking them up in the directory (names, e-mail, team, manager) and applying a team, line manager, Expert flag and grade to all of them. At first sign-in the account is matched by username and completed from the directory; a team or manager set in advance stays locked against the directory. Audited (user_preprovision).

Bulk edit: in Users, tick several people (or filter by team and tick all) and set their team, line manager, Expert flag or grade in one operation; blank fields change nothing. For LDAP users the manager and team you set are locked against the directory by default (untick Lock to let the next login overwrite them). A user who would end up in a hierarchy cycle, or as an Expert in an overtime-only team, is skipped and reported. The operation is audited (users_bulk_update). Teams manages the teams themselves: create, rename (moves every member), delete when empty, switch on-call.

On-call

  • Cover rotates weekly. The rotation starts on {{ week_start_name }} at {{ week_handover }} and ends a week later (Admin › Settings, default Friday 18:00); the days {{ week_start_name }} to {{ week_end_name }} are booked. The week switch books all seven days; a week straddling two months also books the other month's days on that month's sheet.
  • Each day has exactly one type: legal holiday wins over Saturday / Sunday, which win over weekday. The daily stipend comes from the employee's grade (rates per weekday, Saturday, Sunday and holiday).
  • Experts record interventions on the grid (incident number, comment): the daily rate is doubled. Normal employees record interventions as overtime; entries on a booked on-call day are recognised automatically.
  • Conflicts: two employees cannot be on call at the same time. Managers and administrators see a red banner and highlighted days on an on-call sheet, and a tag on the Approvals list, when another employee has the same day on a submitted or approved sheet. Employees outside the manager's team are not named. Drafts are not counted, and approval is not blocked: the manager decides.
  • Teams without on-call: in Admin › Teams on-call can be switched off per team (overtime-only teams). Their members have no On-call page, nav item or on-call line in the Finance export, and their User Guide hides the on-call parts. Existing sheets stay visible; a draft on-call sheet of such a user cannot be edited or submitted. Experts only declare on-call, so a team that has Experts cannot be switched off and an Expert cannot be moved into one. A team without a row, and users without a team, always have on-call.
  • Rates and day types are snapshotted at submission: later rate changes never alter a submitted or approved sheet.

Overtime

  • An entry is a date with from / to times chosen in {{ settings.overtime_step_minutes }}-minute steps. Hours and night hours ({{ '%02d:%02d' % (settings.night_start // 60, settings.night_start % 60) }}–{{ '%02d:%02d' % (settings.night_end // 60, settings.night_end % 60) }}) are computed. An entry past midnight is split into one entry per day; entries of one person may not overlap; future dates are refused.
  • Entries can be logged up to {{ settings.overtime_lookback_months }} months back. They go to the sheet of their own month. If that month is already approved a supplementary sheet (late claim) is opened.
  • Weekend and holiday overtime must be on an on-call day (detected automatically) or ticked pre-approved by the manager; otherwise the sheet cannot be submitted.
  • Compensation is paid or time in lieu (TIL) when the TIL switch is on in Settings. Paid hours map to payroll codes HL100 / HL101 (all), HL111 (night), HL130 (Sunday), HL121 (legal holiday).

Rates & calculation

Day types

Every calendar day has exactly one type: Legal holiday (from the holiday table) wins over Saturday and Sunday, which win over Weekday. A Sunday that is also a holiday is a holiday.

On-call daily rates ({{ settings.currency }} per on-call day, per grade)

The amount of an on-call day is the rate of the employee's grade for that day's type. For an Expert, a day with an intervention pays twice the daily rate, whatever its duration. Normal employees record interventions as overtime instead. Rates are edited under Grades & on-call rates and are frozen on a sheet when it is submitted.

Overtime: hours, day and night

Overtime is entered as a start and end time in {{ settings.overtime_step_minutes }}-minute steps. The application splits each entry into day hours and night hours (night = {{ '%02d:%02d' % (settings.night_start // 60, settings.night_start % 60) }}–{{ '%02d:%02d' % (settings.night_end // 60, settings.night_end % 60) }}) and sums them per day type. The overtime page shows this breakdown for each sheet.

Payroll period

Sheets approved up to the Finance cut-off date of their work month are paid with that month; later approvals are paid with the following month. The cut-off is one explicit date per month, kept in the cut-off calendar (IT Procurement or administrator); a month without a date uses the default pattern, day {{ payroll_cutoff }} of the following month (Settings). See Payroll run & cut-off.

Submission & approval

draft ──submit──▶ submitted ──approve──▶ approved
  ▲                 │  └──reject(comment)──▶ rejected ──(edit)──▶ submit again
  └──withdraw───────┘
approved ──reopen (admin, audited)──▶ draft
  • One submission per month covers the on-call and overtime drafts. If any sheet is refused (for instance unjustified weekend overtime) nothing is submitted.
  • The direct line manager, any manager above them in the hierarchy (skip-level approval) or an administrator approves; nobody approves their own sheet, and peers cannot approve each other. The audit log notes when an approval was made skip-level. Every transition is written to the audit log.
  • Signature (Settings): employees draw, or upload a picture of, their signature and confirm a statement. It is stored with a SHA-256 fingerprint of the sheet; managers see a check that the content is unchanged since signing. This is a simple electronic signature, not a qualified one.
  • Payroll period: sheets approved up to the cut-off date of their work month (calendar, else day {{ payroll_cutoff }} of the following month) are paid with it; later approvals go to the next open payroll period and are shown as late claims (PAYROLL_CUTOFF_DAY).

Finance export

IT Procurement (and administrators) download the reports for a payroll period under Payroll; they cover the whole organisation. Two files follow the layout of the sheets Finance works with:

  • On-call sheet (Y26M10_IT_OnCall.xlsx): one tab per work month. Dates, weekday and type (W, Sat, Sun, H) across the top, employees grouped by team, a 1 on each on-call day, a second "… - Incidents/calls" line for Experts with a 1 on days with an intervention, and the Pricing sheet below with the amount of every day (the rate frozen at submission, so an Expert intervention counts twice). A hidden Support tab holds the holiday list and the day prices.
  • Overtime sheet (Y26M10_IT_Overtime.xlsx): the READ FIRST tab and one tab per work month, with one line per employee and compensation (Paid or TIL), the hours of each day (legal-holiday days are marked LH), the totals Normal / Saturday / Sunday / Legal holiday, the night hours per day type, and the payroll columns HL100, HL101, HL111, HL130, HL121 and TIL.

Each file contains the approved sheets paid in the chosen payroll period. Late claims (earlier work months approved after their cut-off) get their own tab named like the month with "(late)". The cells hold the values computed by the application, not formulas, and a legal holiday that falls on a Saturday or Sunday is counted once, as a holiday. A third download gives a detailed control export (summary of every team member's status, payroll codes and the line-by-line detail).

Payroll run & cut-off

Finance accepts the monthly reports until a cut-off: a date set per month in the cut-off calendar (default pattern: day {{ payroll_cutoff }} of the following month, Admin › Settings). Work of month n is declared in month n+1, so the cut-off of month n+1 applies to it: the July row of the calendar is the date by which June sheets must be approved. The process is controlled in the application:

  1. Employees and managers submit and approve as usual. Everything approved up to the cut-off is paid with its work month.
  2. IT Procurement opens Payroll, chooses the period, and downloads the on-call and overtime reports (whole organisation: the work month plus, one tab per earlier month, everything that was validated late). The page lists what is included, with approval dates, and what is still waiting for approval.
  3. After e-mailing both reports to Finance, IT Procurement ticks the confirmation and records the payroll as sent (optional reference). The application logs it in the audit log, marks every included sheet as sent to Finance (visible to the employee on the sheet) and tells the employees. Recording is only possible when both reports were downloaded and still match the approved data: if something was approved after the download, download again.
  4. The period is now closed. A declaration approved after the cut-off, or after the period was recorded as sent, is paid with the following month. A sheet already sent cannot be reopened.

Notifications. The employee sees a notice (and receives an e-mail when SMTP is configured) when: they submit or log something after the cut-off or for a closed period; their line manager approves it too late; their approved declarations are sent to Finance; or their submitted declaration is still waiting when the period is sent. Notices stay at the top of the pages until dismissed.

The following month, IT Procurement exports that period again and gets the expected month plus everything validated late, each late month in its own tab, with the approval dates listed on the Payroll page.

SSL / HTTPS configuration

TLS is terminated by the proxy container (nginx). The application container is not published; only nginx listens (HTTP redirects to HTTPS, TLS 1.2 / 1.3, HSTS). The certificate is configured with files on the server, not from this interface.

File (in certs/)Content
server.crtServer certificate followed by the intermediate CA chain, PEM, server certificate first.
server.keyPrivate key, PEM, unencrypted, chmod 600.
ldap-ca.pemRoot (and issuing) CA used to validate the domain controllers (LDAPS). Not related to the web certificate.

Option 1: you receive a PFX file (.pfx / .p12) from the PKI

Offline package: run ./convert-pfx.sh server.pfx (Linux) or .\convert-pfx.ps1 server.pfx (Windows) from the installation folder. It asks for the password, writes certs/server.crt (certificate then chain) and certs/server.key using the application image, and shows the names and expiry; then docker compose restart proxy. No openssl is needed. With OpenSSL available, the manual commands are:

The PKI usually generates the key for you and hands over a password-protected PFX containing the key, the certificate and the chain. Convert it with OpenSSL on a trusted machine:

# 1. private key (unencrypted PEM)
openssl pkcs12 -in server.pfx -nocerts -nodes -out certs/server.key
# 2. server certificate, then the chain
openssl pkcs12 -in server.pfx -clcerts -nokeys -out server-leaf.pem
openssl pkcs12 -in server.pfx -cacerts -nokeys -out chain.pem
cat server-leaf.pem chain.pem > certs/server.crt
chmod 600 certs/server.key

OpenSSL 3 refuses PFX files protected with old algorithms (error unsupported): add -legacy to each openssl pkcs12 command. openssl pkcs12 prints "Bag Attributes" headers before each PEM block; they are harmless for nginx, but you can strip them with openssl x509 -in server-leaf.pem -out server-leaf.pem. Delete the PFX and the temporary files afterwards: they contain the private key.

Option 2: you generate the key yourself (recommended)

The key never leaves the server. Create a key and a CSR, submit it to AD CS and assemble the chain:

scripts/make-csr.sh timelog.corp.example.lu [other-dns-name ...]     # certs/server.key + certs/server.csr
certreq -submit -attrib "CertificateTemplate:WebServer" server.csr server.cer   # on a domain-joined Windows host
openssl x509 -inform DER -in server.cer -out server.pem                          # skip if already PEM
cat server.pem issuing-ca.pem > certs/server.crt

Check, apply and verify

# the key and the certificate must match: both lines print the same hash
openssl x509 -in certs/server.crt -noout -pubkey | openssl sha256
openssl pkey -in certs/server.key -pubout | openssl sha256
# subject, alternative names and expiry
openssl x509 -in certs/server.crt -noout -subject -ext subjectAltName -enddate
# apply
docker compose restart proxy
# what clients receive
openssl s_client -connect timelog.corp.example.lu:443 -servername timelog.corp.example.lu < /dev/null | openssl x509 -noout -dates -issuer
  • The certificate's SAN must contain the host name users type (SERVER_NAME in .env).
  • Clients trust the certificate through the domain's root CA, usually distributed by group policy. If browsers warn, the chain in server.crt or the client trust store is the cause.
  • If HTTPS is not published on port 443, set HTTPS_PORT and HTTPS_PORT_SUFFIX=:<port> so the HTTP to HTTPS redirect keeps the port.
  • Renew before the expiry date: repeat the steps and restart the proxy. Short interruption only; no data is affected.
  • Development: scripts/make-selfsigned.sh timelog.local creates a self-signed certificate.

LDAP / Active Directory configuration

Authentication uses the directory for everyone who has no local account. Local accounts are checked first, so a local administrator can always sign in if the directory settings are wrong.

Where to configure

  • In this interface: Admin › Settings › LDAP. Values saved there are stored in the database and override the LDAP_* variables of .env (fields marked "custom"). Reset to .env values removes them.
  • In .env: LDAP_ENABLED=true and the LDAP_* variables (see .env.example), then docker compose up -d.

Fields

SettingMeaning / example
Server URLldaps://dc01.corp.example.lu:636, or ldap://dc01:389 with StartTLS. Use LDAPS or StartTLS: credentials must not travel in clear text.
CA certificatePaste the PKI root (and issuing) CA as PEM, or provide certs/ldap-ca.pem (path /certs/ldap-ca.pem in the container). Keep "Verify the server certificate" on.
Bind DN / passwordA read-only service account used to search for users. The password is encrypted with a key derived from SECRET_KEY and never displayed; rotating SECRET_KEY means re-entering it.
User search base / filterFor example OU=Users,DC=corp,DC=example,DC=lu and (sAMAccountName={username}). The filter must contain {username}.
Attribute mappingDefaults suit AD: sAMAccountName, givenName, sn, displayName, mail, department (team), manager.
GroupsDNs of the groups for Allowed to sign in, Administrators and Expert employees. Nested membership is resolved by AD. If a group is empty, that flag is managed by hand in Users.

How login works

  1. The service account searches for the user; the user's own password is then checked with a bind. Empty passwords are refused.
  2. On each login the user is created or updated: names, e-mail, team and the line manager (the manager entry is created too, so the hierarchy exists before they log in), unless the manager is locked.
  3. Group membership sets the administrator / Expert flags and decides who may log in.

Test and go live

  1. Save the settings, then use Test connection: it connects, binds with the service account and, if you give a username, shows that person's attributes, groups and manager. No user password is needed.
  2. Sign in with a directory account in a private window.
  3. When it works, set LOCAL_LOGIN_ADMIN_ONLY=true so that only local administrators can still use local passwords (keep one local break-glass administrator).

Typical failures: cannot connect (firewall, port, name resolution), certificate verify failed (missing CA or wrong host name in the URL), invalid credentials (bind DN or password), no entry (search base or filter).

Operations

  • Backup: Admin › Backup creates, downloads, uploads, deletes and restores backups, and sets the schedule (daily or weekly, time in UTC, number of automatic backups to keep). Every backup is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) with a key derived from BACKUP_PASSPHRASE, or from SECRET_KEY when that is not set: store the passphrase safely and separately, a backup cannot be read without it. Files live in the backups/ folder next to docker-compose.yml: download or copy them off the host regularly, a backup on the same disk does not protect against losing the server. A restore replaces the whole database and writes a safety copy first. scripts/backup-db.sh (a native PostgreSQL dump, not encrypted by the app) remains available for cron.
  • Logs: docker compose logs -f app proxy. Health: GET /healthz.
  • Update: scripts/build.sh (or load the new image with docker load), then docker compose up -d. The footer shows the running version and build time (build {{ build.version }}).
  • Secrets live in .env (git-ignored): SECRET_KEY, BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_PASSWORD, database password. The app refuses to start with the example placeholders.
  • Settings in the Settings page switch features on or off: time in lieu, signature on submission, amounts visible to employees.
